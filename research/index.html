Research

I develop and apply computational methods — first-principles calculations, exciton theory, semi-empirical pseudopotentials, and quantum transport — to understand the electronic, optical, and transport properties of low-dimensional materials.

Recent Publications (2026)

Hybridization-Revealed Interlayer 2p Exciton State in Bilayer MoS₂

S. Cha, Z. Xu, T. Ouyang, H. Yao, R. K. Paudel, T. Taniguchi, K. Watanabe, A. Y. Joe, Y.-C. Chang, N. M. Gabor, C. H. Lui — Nature Physics (accepted, 2026). Preprint

Beyond the 1s ground state, bilayer MoS₂ hosts a full Rydberg series of interlayer excitons from 1s to 4s, together with a 2p interlayer state. The 2p exciton is dark at zero field; an applied electric field mixes it with the intralayer A exciton, and it becomes optically visible. While the intralayer series fades beyond 2s, the interlayer series remains visible up to 4s.

Brightening Interlayer Excitons by Electric-Field-Driven Hole Transfer in Bilayer WSe₂

T. Ouyang, E. Liu, S. Cha, R. Cang, R. K. Paudel, Y. Sun, Z. Xu, T. Taniguchi, K. Watanabe, N. M. Gabor, Y.-C. Chang, C. H. Lui — Physical Review Letters 136, 096903 (2026), Editors' Suggestion. DOI · arXiv

Under an applied electric field, reflectance-contrast spectroscopy resolves the interlayer A1s, A2s and B1s excitons of bilayer WSe₂. Our DFT calculations show that the field distorts the valence-band Bloch states and moves the hole from one layer to the other. The interlayer excitons thereby gain intralayer character and oscillator strength without needing to hybridize with bright intralayer states — a general brightening mechanism for bilayer TMDs whenever inter- and intralayer excitons are well separated in energy.

Semi-Empirical Pseudopotential Method for Monolayer Transition-Metal Dichalcogenides

R. K. Paudel, C.-Y. Ren, Y.-C. Chang — Computer Physics Communications 327, 110240 (2026). DOI · arXiv

We extended SEPM to monolayer TMDCs. The method gives DFT-quality band structures at a fraction of the cost and isolates the electronic ingredients that control optical response and exciton formation.

Current Work: Charge Transfer and Quantum Transport in F4TCNQ–Graphene

I study charge transfer and transport in F4TCNQ molecules adsorbed on graphene. Using large graphene supercells (e.g., 16 × 16), we follow how molecular adsorption modifies graphene's electronic structure, redistributes charge, and changes its transport characteristics.

The calculations combine density functional theory with non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) transport to obtain transmission spectra and conductance, and to identify how molecular dopants act in graphene-based devices. This work is carried out in the group of Prof. Ming-Hao Liu at NCKU.

  • DFT-based electronic structure of molecule–graphene interfaces
  • Charge-density difference and electron-transfer analysis
  • NEGF-based transmission and conductance calculations
  • Design principles for molecularly functionalized graphene devices

Semi-Empirical Pseudopotential Method (SEPM)

A major part of my work is the development of the Semi-Empirical Pseudopotential Method (SEPM), an efficient and accurate way to compute electronic structures for large and complex systems. In our implementation the in-plane coordinates are treated with plane waves, while the out-of-plane direction is expanded in B-spline basis functions. The method includes local and non-local potential terms and is parameterized to reproduce first-principles DFT results and, where available, experimental data.

This work is carried out in collaboration with Prof. Chung-Yuan Ren (NKNU).

Graphene and Graphene Nanoribbons

SEPM captures the Dirac-cone physics of graphene and the width-dependent band gaps of armchair graphene nanoribbons (aGNRs), useful for understanding nanoelectronic behavior in carbon-based materials: Nanomaterials 13, 2066 (2023).

Future Directions

Future research will focus on computational frameworks that bridge electronic structure, many-body physics, and quantum transport in low-dimensional materials. One direction is extending SEPM to large-scale 2D materials and van der Waals heterostructures, where accurate interlayer coupling, moiré potentials, and electric-field effects open access to new electronic phases and excitonic phenomena in twisted bilayers.

Another direction connects materials modeling with devices: combining DFT with NEGF to explore charge transfer, molecular doping, and transport in functionalized 2D materials, including graphene-based molecular interfaces. The goal is computational tools that predict electronic, optical, and transport properties of quantum materials and guide the design of nanoelectronic and optoelectronic devices.
